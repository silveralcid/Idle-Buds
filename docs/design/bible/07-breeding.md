## Breeding

Breeding is a reliable, measurable route to desirable creatures, not a universally superior route. Wild hunting and breeding should both be capable of exceptional results. Hunters supply fresh specimens and discoveries; breeders refine traits and offer more consistent results; battlers, producers and collectors create demand. Breeders should continue to find value in the wider ecosystem rather than making catching irrelevant through perfect, infinitely reproducible lines.

Breeding is accessible relatively early, but exceptional bloodlines and professional breeding mastery are deliberate slow-burn, late-game pursuits. The first breeding experience should teach inheritance through a rewarding, approachable result, without requiring prolonged combat progression. Breeding needs meaningful decisions and constraints to avoid oversupply and routine maintenance fatigue. Simple inheritance is the initial direction. Exact parent rules, costs, incubation, genetic mechanisms and ways to preserve wild value are unresolved. Complex genetics, fusion and encounter boosters belong in the incubator until qualified. Owner-approved breeding direction (November 25, 2026): combine Palworld-inspired cross-species inheritance and Pokémon-inspired compatibility constraints, prioritizing simplicity, easy comprehension, idle suitability and a healthy player-driven Bud economy. Same-species breeding and compatible cross-species breeding are the design direction; players should be able to understand eligibility and expected species before committing, while individual genetics remain variable. Do not assume Palworld's hidden breeding-power calculation, a large manually maintained pair table, Pokémon's exact egg groups or sex rules, or arbitrary rare-species creation. Owner approved the simple parent-species model: players select a primary parent whose species determines offspring species; both compatible parents contribute IVs and inherited traits, and the UI previews the offspring species before commitment. Same-species pairs naturally retain their species. This could support cross-species trait/IV transfer without letting two common parents create an unrelated rare species. Exact compatibility grouping, rare-species restrictions, other parent eligibility and genetic inheritance probabilities are still open. Special pairings producing a third species are a deferred candidate, not a baseline requirement. No inheritance probabilities, production costs, timers or release scope approved. Owner-approved breeding eligibility (November 25, 2026): Option C, hybrid. Most Buds use the traditional male/female pair requirement alongside species compatibility. Rare, unusual or unique species may have explicitly defined exceptions; rarity alone does not automatically confer an exception. Either eligible parent may still be designated the primary parent determining offspring species; the primary-parent choice is not dictated by sex. Exact sex distributions, exceptional species and their special eligibility rules, and any other parent restrictions remain open. No universal genderless rule, sex-changing item or new mechanic approved. Owner-approved breeding decisions Q3–Q6 (November 25, 2026): Q3 A+C, interpreted as time-only baseline breeding with optional crafted enhancements; no universal mandatory breeding consumable. Preserve time/capacity as supply constraints and evaluate optional boosts and economic effects later; exact enhancement effects, costs, recipes and eligibility remain open. This interpretation is disclosed for owner correction if needed. Q4 B, each of the four IVs (Attack, Health, Gather, Craft) is independently selected from either eligible parent or generated randomly according to future balanced probabilities. Do not require averaging or dominant/recessive genetic simulation. Q5 B, inherit eligible parental traits with a chance of new randomly generated traits; exact trait limits, compatibility restrictions, rarity weights, inheritance probabilities, and random-generation pools remain open. Neither exact parental IV selection weights nor guaranteed inherited values are fixed. Q6 B, breeding produces an egg, followed by incubation before a Bud hatches; wild eggs can reuse the incubation system. No mandatory separate juvenile maturation stage. Exact breeding/incubation durations, concurrent slots, repeat/queue behavior, overflow, breeding eligibility, whether enhancements affect time/genetics and supply balancing remain open. Six-question breeding interview complete. No release scope or implementation approval.
