# 1\. Game identity

**Pitch:** An idle-first monster MMO where players catch, raise, breed, deploy and trade unique creatures, build mastery at their own pace, and find their place in a living multiplayer economy. Collectors, breeders, merchants, skillers and competitive battlers can all pursue meaningful success.

**Player fantasy:** Build a life with monsters in a living multiplayer world, at your own pace. Develop creatures you care about, set your own goals, and enjoy accomplishments that matter to other players.

**Design hierarchy:** Idle-first gameplay is the governing constraint; monster taming supplies the theme, identity and much of the depth; multiplayer is the principal long-term differentiator. When a proposed monster feature conflicts with accessible idle play, adapt the feature rather than compromising the idle experience.

**Audience:** Busy players who enjoy idle RPG progression, collecting, optimization and social games, including casual newcomers and ambitious veterans. Design for the casual majority first without limiting the ambitions of dedicated optimizers. The owner's 80/20 framing is a design heuristic, not a measured market split.

## Three foundational pillars

**1\. Progress at your own pace.** Satisfying, accessible idle progression that respects real-life responsibilities. Easy to learn, difficult to master. The game should be enjoyable even before monster collection or multiplayer systems are introduced.

**2\. Meaningful multiplayer.** Trade, cooperate and compete in a persistent community. Player interactions amplify the meaning of personal achievements, and cooperation makes progression more efficient without forcing players into a prescribed role.

**3\. Monsters as partners in progress (provisional wording).** Creatures are desirable for emotional attachment, collection, combat, breeding and productive utility. The trainer can act independently, but acquiring and developing monsters makes their capabilities substantially greater. The underlying principle is accepted; the final pillar wording remains open.

**Not the game:** A traditional open-world or manual-combat MMO, a narrative-heavy adventure, a mandatory daily grind, a class-locked progression system, or a game that requires spreadsheets to enjoy. Avoid adding breadth merely to simulate every aspect of monster ownership.
