# 6\. Commercial design

**Monetization is inspired by IdleOn: low-friction, affordable one-time purchases, permanent account value, earnable premium currency and supporter/update bundles. The owner expressly accepts paid acceleration, provided features and completion are not gated behind payment. No mandatory subscription is the preferred starting direction. A small first purchase around \$5 is a pricing hypothesis, not a fixed catalog. Owner-approved commercial direction (November 25, 2026): Q1 A, free-to-play. Q2 mainly B with some A, primarily permanent-value upgrades with a limited role for consumable purchases; no exact consumable category or quantity approved. Q3 B, premium currency both earnable through play and purchasable. Q4 B+A, earnable gameplay upgrades/paid acceleration alongside cosmetics, never pay-exclusive gameplay power or completion. Q5 A, premium currency account-bound/non-player-tradeable. Q6 B, permanent shop plus occasional supporter bundles; seasonal battle passes and mandatory subscription not part of the approved direction. All functional upgrades must have meaningful attainable free-play paths; exact catalog, prices, caps, availability, consumable effects, premium earning/spending rates, economic safeguards and launch timing remain open. Do not equate earnable paid acceleration with automatic competitive fairness. No monetization implementation or release-scope approval. Owner clarified in the release-direction interview: monetization only after actual official launch, not during pre-launch testing or public beta. Do not plan a monetized beta; exact timing after official launch and offerings remain open.**

Free players must have a genuine, achievable gameplay route to functional progression and upgrades. Paid purchases may accelerate progress and provide convenience or cosmetic distinction, but must never make features or completion pay-exclusive. The exact earn rates, prices, upgrade caps, paid/earned parity, purchase catalogue, allowed consumable effects and economic safeguards are unresolved. Premium currency is now approved as account-bound and non-player-tradeable.

Competitive and economic integrity is a design constraint, not a claim that paid acceleration is harmless. Any paid benefit that changes resource output, breeding supply, access to monsters or combat effectiveness must be assessed against market fairness and new-player competitiveness. Design the limits before enabling paid progression in a live economy. Avoid selling earned prestige itself. The previous research log's stricter cosmetic-only recommendations are historical proposals, superseded by the owner's current acceptance of paid acceleration.
