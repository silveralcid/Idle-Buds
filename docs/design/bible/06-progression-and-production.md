## Current owner decision: simpler production and storage (November 26, 2026)

This playtest decision is approved for the Experience MVP and supersedes the older node-exclusivity, location restriction, remote-relay and individual item-cap paragraphs retained below.

- Start any eligible activity directly. Access becomes available automatically when its existing combat or production requirements are met; no manual travel or unlock command is needed.
- Gather and patrol continuously until stopped. Crafting supports a finite quantity or continuous work. A valid replacement immediately discards the previous incomplete cycle and its uncompleted queue, retaining all already completed output.
- Assign **one Bud per production skill**, including one Crafting Bud for the whole major crafting area. Buds work without the Tender participating. The Tender and one Bud can work on the same resource simultaneously. Replacing the helper unassigns the old one automatically.
- Preserve earned total deployment capacity while limiting every skill to one Bud. Combat formation and breeding commitments retain their existing restrictions and do not become production-helper slots.
- Tender mastery and species aptitude determine work eligibility. Bud level changes efficiency, never repeats the Tender's resource-level gate. Reuse the existing modest general-level, IV, trait and bond effects; no separate per-Bud skill XP tracks.
- Explicit pause or stop remains deliberate. A job blocked by inputs or storage resumes automatically when its requirements become available, including during offline event reconciliation.
- Inventory has shared slots: one slot per fungible item type with unlimited quantity, and one per unique unequipped equipment instance. Equipped weapon/armor, Bud collection and eggs have separate treatment.
- If space fills during a cycle, retain its completed roll without loss or reroll and pause that lane until delivery is possible. Keep one pending completed output per Tender/skill lane, independent of worker replacement or stopping. Do not build a general overflow mailbox.
- Current numerical baseline: 24 inventory slots plus 8 per earned storage upgrade. This is provisional economy tuning, not a new commercial decision. Preserve over-capacity legacy possessions; existing stacks can grow while new slot-requiring additions wait.

Geography remains world identity, encounter content and progression context. Remove the Remote Relay requirement and compensate earlier purchases once. Specific efficiency curves, deployment pacing and storage costs remain future economic evaluation.

> Reconciliation: later owner decisions in this section supersede older generic unknowns: Tender skills and individual Bud levels cap at 99; Melee, Ranged, Resonance and Vitality are the four conceptual Tender combat skills; the derived Combat Level was removed. Combat gear has a narrow approved variability direction (weapons: Attack and compatible technique; armor: Health and compatible passive), while routine goods stay predictable. Permanent equipment has no durability or mandatory repairs. Exact formulas and effects remain open; this does not approve implementation.

## Trainer skills and monster progression

**Palworld is the primary design reference for the overall Bud system, including monster typing, creature skills, work suitabilities, species differentiation and individual development. Draw broadly from its creature-system model and adapt it to an idle MMO rather than merely borrowing its production automation. Capture, training and breeding design should use it as a reference while honoring our established design principles. Specific types, skills, mechanics and balance values are not yet approved; this direction does not import Palworld's complete feature list or enlarge release scope. Owner-approved foundational Bud model (November 25, 2026): (1) Species Ability: a species-linked passive or triggered effect, potentially affecting combat, gathering and/or production; species and breeding determine ability identity or expression, with exact inheritance and eligibility rules still undecided. (2) Learned Technique: one selectable learned effect added to a universal normal frontline hit based directly on Attack; reuse the existing triggered-ability rules, without a separate move-power statistic or action timing system. Species compatibility, individual Bud training, Tender progression and earned unlocks determine the available move pool; both Tender and Bud progression contribute. Exact move acquisition, equipping, capacity, transfer and activation rules remain open. (3) Inherited Traits: individual passive qualities from shared and species-exclusive pools of differing rarities, affecting combat, gathering and/or production; breeding enables inheritance and combination. Wild-caught specimens can also possess useful individual qualities, consistent with the approved catching direction. (4) Work Aptitudes: species-defined natural eligibility and base suitability for work, with individual development affecting effectiveness; owner subsequently selected fixed species aptitudes, with individual level, genetics and traits improving performance; do not add permanent base-aptitude upgrades or learnable new aptitudes to the baseline. Tender skill mastery still controls activity/resource-tier access, without duplicate per-Bud skill XP tracks. Palworld reference: work suitabilities are species-defined but can improve via condensation, technique books and temporary partner effects (https\://palworld.wiki.gg/wiki/Working); no such upgrade systems are imported automatically. (5) Level: individual experience progression. (6) Attack and (7) Health: derived from species, level and applicable modifiers, the initial readable core battle statistics. Exact formulas, caps, IV effects, additional stats, trait/ability counts and breeding details remain open. (8) Typing: owner-approved provisional type-based synergy model (November 25, 2026), inspired by Magic: The Gathering's color identities and deck-building tradeoffs, not its mana system or deck-construction rules. Types serve as recognizable strategic identities and ability/technique synergy tags rather than a universal elemental effectiveness chart. A single-type team should naturally find cohesive synergies; mixed-type teams remain viable through thoughtful composition, complementary abilities and positioning. Do not impose a mandatory mono-type bonus, hard composition restrictions, a separate type resource/mana system, or automatic damage multipliers merely because of typing. Type synergies are expressed through existing ability/technique/trait rules. Owner-approved single typing (November 25, 2026): each Bud species has exactly one type. Mixed-type teams are allowed and encouraged where complementary synergies justify them; no dual-type Buds in the current design. Exact type identities, number, crossover synergies and balance remain undecided. Evaluate strategic variety and whether mono-type and mixed-type builds are both meaningful in the future combat sandbox. Held items are a separate candidate in the Idea Incubator, not an approved equipment system. This is a foundational design promotion, not release scope or permission to implement. Owner requests SAP-like low numerical scale for Attack and Health: prioritize small, easily compared and mentally tractable values, avoiding large-number inflation. Integer presentation and modest growth are recommended; exact ranges, progression curves, rounding and caps are not approved. Owner-approved provisional attack expression (November 25, 2026): Option B, universal standard frontline hit based directly on current Attack, with one selectable learned technique expressed as a concise additional attack effect through the shared triggered-ability rules. No separate move-power statistic, action-selection AI, mana, accuracy rolls, cooldowns or move rotations. Retain species compatibility, individual Bud training, Tender progression and earned technique unlocks. Preserve Option A (plain stat-based hit without learned technique effects) as the simplest initial post-gate sandbox baseline and fallback if B does not provide meaningful fun/customization for its added complexity. Option C (fully distinct active move replacement) is cataloged but not selected. Low, mentally tractable Attack/Health numbers are the preferred baseline; exact ranges, caps, rounding, growth formulas, available techniques and trigger/damage order remain open. Owner-approved genetic potential model (November 25, 2026): exactly four inheritable numerical IV categories, Attack, Health, Gather and Craft, alongside the existing inherited-trait system. Attack and Health affect combat-stat potential; Gather covers Mining, Woodcutting, Fishing and Farming; Craft covers Crafting, Cooking and Synthesis, not only the Crafting skill. Species-defined aptitude continues to determine eligible jobs and base suitability, while IVs modify performance within eligibility; inherited traits express qualitative specialization, rare combinations and tradeoffs. No additional Defense IV, separate speed/yield/material-efficiency IVs or individual skill IVs in the approved model. Other models retained as historical alternatives, not approved features. Defense remains only a possible later combat-testing alternative. Exact IV scale/range, stat/efficiency formulas, trait-slot count, inheritance and mutation rules remain open. No separate Tender genetic system; detailed Tender combat discussion deferred. Design approval does not approve release scope or implementation. Historical comparison and rationale (see Research Log Sections 12.17–12.19 and Idea Incubator): two IVs (Attack/Health), three IVs (combined Work), four IVs (selected), six IVs (four distinct work-rate/yield/efficiency dimensions), and nine IVs (per-skill work potentials). Owner rejected the more granular models for excess complexity; preserve the selected four-stat genetic model. Combat remains Attack/Health based, with small readable numbers. Genetic depth should come from useful inherited traits and inheritance rather than adding more numerical dimensions.**

**Use complementary progression, not mirrored skill trees. Trainers have broad RuneScape/Melvor-style individual skill progression. Buds have natural species-specific aptitudes with room for improvement within their capabilities, drawing from Palworld's work suitability model. Some species may be unsuited to particular jobs. Individual training should matter without requiring every Bud to maintain the same complete set of skills as the trainer. Owner-approved XP ownership (November 25, 2026): Trainer skill XP comes from the trainer's own activities; Bud labor grants XP to the Bud only, not to the trainer. Buds also develop through combat. Buds expand productive capacity rather than trainer skill XP capacity and can eventually become significantly more efficient than a trainer at specialized work. Exact XP curves and the form of Bud development remain open.**

The trainer can carry out normal production activities without Buds. Buds expand the trainer's productive capabilities by performing activities concurrently, within the same profession or across different professions. Production is node-based: each personal resource or production node allows one assigned entity at a time, whether trainer or Bud. No worker stacking or shared-node assistance in the initial design; nodes are not contested globally between players. The number of simultaneously deployed Buds is limited by trainer progression. Exact deployment capacity and node catalog remain undecided. Owner-approved deployment progression (November 25, 2026): Hybrid (Option C). Trainer/account progression unlocks eligibility for additional Bud deployment capacity, and players then earn or craft permanent capacity upgrades using resources. Portal-powered coordination technology provides a thematic direction for those upgrades. Keep the mechanism simple; no separate base-building or deployment-management skill is required. Exact milestone criteria, acquisition methods, costs, capacity limits, and how combat/breeding assignments interact with production slots remain unresolved. This does not approve a paid-capacity model or additional release scope.

Access and efficiency are separate: trainer mastery unlocks activities and resource tiers, while a Bud's natural aptitude and individual development determine its eligibility for that kind of work and its effectiveness. A powerful Bud cannot bypass a trainer's locked resource tier. Once the trainer unlocks a node, a Bud with the appropriate work aptitude can generally use it without repeating the trainer's skill grind or meeting a duplicate resource-level requirement. Bud development primarily improves performance. These principles apply to gathering and crafting generally; exact ratings, formulas, detailed resource/recipe trees and caps remain open. Tender skills and individual Buds both have an owner-approved level cap of 99; their XP curves and time to mastery may differ. Owner-approved skill framework (November 25, 2026): Mining, Woodcutting, Fishing, Farming, Cooking, Crafting and Synthesis are the seven owner-approved foundational noncombat skills. Crafting consolidates material processing, tools, equipment, electronics and capture devices into one skill with recipe categories; Synthesis covers medicine, chemical compounds and specialized consumables. The owner favors consolidated skills for simpler UX, onboarding, progression and system complexity. Split professions only when gameplay evidence establishes a meaningful benefit. Specific recipes, item categories and unlocks remain open; no additional systems or release scope are approved by these examples. Trainer combat progression is required, but its skill structure remains open pending future bounded combat experimentation. Catching and breeding are systems/activities rather than standalone skills in the current design direction; exploration is a feature/activity first, with a dedicated skill considered only if meaningful mastery warrants it. A skill must justify its existence through meaningful mastery, unlocks and specialization; otherwise express it as an activity or system. These are design decisions, not an approved release list. Owner-approved production dependencies (November 25, 2026): Option B with selective C for advanced items. Keep early-game recipes simple and approachable; introduce moderate processing and cross-skill dependencies as progression develops, with selectively deeper production chains for advanced items where they create meaningful economic or strategic value. Avoid turning the game into an industrial logistics simulator or requiring unnecessary intermediate components. Recipe examples are illustrative, not approved items, formulas or release scope. Owner-approved operating-cost direction (November 25, 2026): Option C, with activity-specific exceptions. Buds do not require universal mandatory feeding or upkeep to perform ordinary work. Optional food and other consumables can improve performance and create recurring economic demand. Particular activities may require appropriate inputs or consumables depending on what the Bud is doing. Exact required inputs, enhancement effects, consumption rates and activity-specific failure or fallback behavior remain undecided. Avoid routine care chores that undermine idle-first play.

Owner-approved noncombat skill responsibilities (November 25, 2026): Mining yields ores, minerals and crystals for equipment, technology and Synthesis. Woodcutting yields timber, fibers and resins for tools, equipment and Crafting. Fishing yields fish and aquatic materials for Cooking and Synthesis. Farming yields crops, herbs and organic materials for Cooking, Synthesis and potential breeding enhancements. Cooking produces food and beverages for temporary performance bonuses. Crafting produces equipment, tools and capture devices, including the consolidated processing and technology categories already approved. Synthesis produces medicine, chemicals and compounds for specialized enhancements and advanced recipes. Each skill should be independently worthwhile and support interconnected player-market demand. Exact items, recipes, quantities, unlocks and balancing remain open. Existing seven noncombat skills remain approved. Owner explicitly selected keeping portal technology within Crafting for now rather than adding Engineering as an eighth noncombat skill. Use recipe categories for energy technology, capture supplies, Resonance equipment and coordination technology as appropriate; examples are not approved individual recipes. Separate Engineering and other Melvor-inspired skill concepts remain incubated for later evidence-based review. The seven noncombat and four combat skills remain the current lineup. Optional Resonance energy consumables and other unselected Engineering interview options are not approved. No detailed recipe commitments, release scope or implementation authorization.
