# 2\. Player experience and retention

## Routine and active play

**Routine loop: Set activities → progress while away → check outcomes → enjoy rewards and discoveries → make decisions aligned with personal goals → assign the next activities. A routine daily check-in should fit in 5–10 minutes, across playstyles.**

**Onboarding: Teach through play. Start with goal-driven discovery, gradually transitioning into contextual guidance and open sandbox play. Early objectives naturally teach relevant mechanics through meaningful, permanent progression. The initial journey is recommended, not a mandatory tutorial sequence; do not introduce unrelated systems before they become relevant.**

**First-session target (owner approved): Within approximately five minutes, a new player should earn their first Bud; within 10–15 minutes, understand the basic loop and set up meaningful offline progression. This is the first satisfying opportunity to log out, not a limit on playtime. Players who want to continue should have meaningful exploration, catching, crafting and optimization opportunities without being forced to wait. Teach only what is needed now.**

**Pacing (owner approved, Option C): Fast, rewarding onboarding followed by expanding capabilities and opportunities, leaning toward Palworld-style empowerment rather than simply lengthening timers. Longer waits may create anticipation when appropriate, but waiting must not be the only productive option. Idle-first, not idle-only. Exact timings, unlocks and activity limits remain undecided.**

**There is no single mandatory morning sequence.** A collector checks new catches, a breeder checks incubation or offspring, a merchant checks sales, a battler checks asynchronous battle outcomes, and a skiller checks production. Players can do any combination. The owner's personal preference for breeding and markets is an example, not the default for every player.

Deeper engagement comes from planning, team building, economic decisions, collection goals and optimization. External spreadsheets and specialized tools may be enjoyable for the most competitive players, but must not be a prerequisite for ordinary play. More effort should earn an advantage, without requiring everyone to remain online continuously. The exact active-play advantage and diminishing-return mechanisms remain undecided.

**Offline progression (owner approved, November 25, 2026): Universal 24-hour offline cap displayed clearly to players and enforced, with a small grace period beyond the nominal cap to accommodate slightly late daily check-ins. Full normal activity efficiency while eligible; no indefinite diminishing-return progression beyond the cap and grace. Natural activity-specific constraints still apply: consumed inputs, completed finite activities and other legitimate activity requirements can end work sooner. The exact grace duration and renewal/resumption behavior remain open for balancing. The intent is reliable daily habits and anticipation without punitive scheduling or monetizing intolerable baseline restrictions.**

## Why return tomorrow?

**Intrinsic satisfaction:** Rising numbers, meaningful rewards, improving efficiency and growing mastery must feel good on their own. Monster attachment and multiplayer build on that base.

**Anticipation:** Players should look forward to discovering results, such as a valuable catch, a rare egg hatching, a successful sale or a battle outcome. Timers should be long enough to make rewards exciting without blocking other enjoyable activity. The sixteen-hour legendary egg was an illustrative example, not an approved timer.

**Ambition:** Self-selected goals give weeks, months and years of progression meaning. Collection completion, exceptional specimens, wealth, team mastery, skill mastery and community standing are all valid accomplishments.

## Long-term progression

Idle Buds is intended to be an enduring live game, not a product that players must repeatedly repurchase as sequels. The owner wants a lasting creative outlet and commercial opportunity through content updates, events and new experiences over time.

**Favor emergent longevity first:** the owner described roughly 60–70% reliance on interconnected, self-renewing systems and 20–30% on new authored content. These are directional estimates, not an exhaustive allocation or a production quota. New content refreshes the ecosystem; it should not be required to keep the underlying gameplay interesting. Complexity through interaction is preferable to complexity through feature count.

**Veteran/newcomer balance:** Long-term effort, wealth, collections and prestige should matter, but early arrival must not guarantee permanent dominance. New players need a plausible path to meaningful and competitive participation. Equal cumulative effort is a useful fairness benchmark, not a promise of identical progression regardless of calendar time. Exact catch-up, power scaling and competitive rules are unresolved. Endless opportunities are the goal; endless vertical power is not a requirement.
