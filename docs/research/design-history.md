# 2\. Confirmed direction and decision register

* Working title: Idle Buds. This replaces the proposed Fofofadi name for planning purposes; final commercial name is undecided.  
* Genre: persistent, interface-first idle monster-collecting MMORPG. Combine Koltera's creature collecting with Melvor Idle / Idle Clans-style UI and skilling, and Idle Clans-style social multiplayer.  
* Core differentiators: a player market for individual monsters, breeding, and strategic automated PvP in both asynchronous and synchronous formats. The latter two PvP formats are part of the long-term vision, not a commitment to launch together.  
* Platform direction: desktop-first, with strong mobile support and shared progression across platforms.  
* Art target: original creature pixel sprites with the visual readability and approximate complexity of Pokémon Emerald / GBA-era artwork. Use the era as a quality benchmark, not Pokémon designs, names, assets or trade dress.  
* Monetization direction: the owner liked the revised IdleOn-inspired approach of permanent account value, earnable premium currency and supporter bundles rather than relying on mandatory subscriptions. Exact catalog, prices and balance rules remain proposals.

Pitch: Collect, breed, train and trade unique creatures in a persistent multiplayer world. Master interconnected skills, develop valuable bloodlines, build wealth and compete in strategic auto-battles while your ordinary progression continues offline.

# 3\. Market opportunity and research findings

The opportunity is an idle MMO for people who want long-term progression, collecting, optimization, trading and social competition without the time commitment or production burden of a traditional MMORPG. Adjacent games validate parts of this formula, not demand for this exact combination. The monster market, breeding and auto-battles should create a mutually reinforcing reason to return.

# 4\. Gameplay pillars and core loop

Four connected pillars: idle progression; creature collection and breeding; a player-driven MMO economy; strategic automated combat. The economic ecosystem is the product's distinguishing feature, not a traditional RPG with cosmetic pets attached.

* Explore and catch: choose zones, encounter species, catch creatures, discover rare appearances, fill the collection index and find useful specimens.  
* Train and breed: raise levels, improve bloodlines, evolve creatures, discover inherited traits and mutations. Keep the initial genetics model simple.  
* Deploy: use creatures in PvE, PvP and productive activities. Species can specialize in combat, gathering, crafting or other utility so non-combat monsters remain desirable.  
* Earn and trade: gather resources, craft consumables and gear, sell materials or individual creatures, purchase breeding inputs and reinvest in progression.  
* Upgrade and repeat: unlock additional zones, skills, species, recipes and team options; maintain meaningful idle progression even when the player is offline.

## Skill and monster progression (proposals)

Separate trainer skills from individual creature stats. Trainer mastery unlocks content and recipes; creature quality determines efficiency, suitability and strategic options. Candidate skills include exploration, taming, breeding, gathering/mining, farming, crafting, fishing, cooking and alchemy. Select only five interconnected skills for the first release. Do not commit to the full list.

Each creature can have a species, level, evolution stage, individual genetic attributes, passive traits, rarity/appearance and combat or gathering specialization. Distinct specimens of the same species should have different market value. Prefer legible inheritance and bounded modifiers over complex genetics or a large combinatorial ruleset at first.

## Player economy and breeding

Provide a resource exchange and an individual-monster marketplace. Listings should expose species, level, key stats, genetics, traits, appearance and asking price. Search and filters make the market useful. Collectors, hunters, breeders, skillers, merchants and battlers should all be viable identities.

Basic breeding requires parents, resource costs, incubation time and a comprehensible chance of inheritance/mutation. Advanced pedigree records, stud rental and complex dominance/recessiveness are later ideas. Control supply with meaningful resource sinks, cooldowns, limited breeding capacity and optional creature-consuming progression where appropriate. Avoid forcing emotionally attached players to destroy favorites.

Risks: infinite production causes inflation; infinitely reproducible monsters lose value; wealth can dominate PvP; low player population makes a market illiquid. Design meaningful sinks, server-authoritative ownership and transfers, duplicate prevention, anti-abuse checks, NPC fallbacks and enjoyable self-sufficient progression. These are design considerations, not an implementation task.

## Auto-battles and PvP

* PvE: automated team encounters against enemies and bosses, with loot and progression. The player chooses the team and strategy before battle.  
* Asynchronous PvP: challenge saved defense teams while opponents are offline; rankings, battle logs/replays and configurable lineups. Proposed first competitive release because it works at low concurrent population.  
* Synchronous PvP: live matchmaking and spectated auto-battles with both players present. Long-term feature. Reuse the same battle rules rather than developing separate combat systems.  
* Possible strategic inputs: team composition, abilities, equipment, targeting priorities and formation. Avoid excessive ability count and complex manual controls.  
* Fairness: counters, species roles, team rules and diminishing returns on perfect genetics. Evaluate normalization or distinct ranked formats if wealth overwhelms strategy. No purchased power should decide rankings.

# 5\. Roguelite concept (post-launch backlog)

Monster Expedition / Wild Expedition: a suspendable, 15–30 minute run in which a player starts with one owned monster (or a standardized starter), recruits a temporary team, chooses branching encounters and acquires temporary relics or mutations. Battles reuse the auto-battle system. On defeat, the run resets; permanent owned creatures are never lost.

* Encounter types: battles, elite encounters, nests, shops, shrines, treasure, bosses. Choices between fights create active engagement when players want more than idle timers.  
* Temporary effects may create powerful combinations that would be inappropriate in the persistent economy: extra traits, lifesteal, shared buffs, revives, elemental fusion and temporary evolution.  
* Permanent rewards: carefully controlled eggs, mutation catalysts, fossils, crafting materials, tokens, cosmetics and achievements. Do not make constant active grinding the optimal way to dominate the idle economy.  
* Later ideas: standardized draft arena and asynchronous competitive snapshots; endless abyss. All expedition content is deferred until the main loop is validated.

# 6\. Art, content and budget

Target GBA / Pokémon Emerald-era pixel art: distinctive silhouettes, expressive creatures, limited palettes and readable detail at small sizes. Default to a single static front/three-quarter battle sprite per design, transparent background, consistent scale and palette conventions. A simple generic animation/effect system can convey attacks, damage and status without dedicated animation sheets. Front/back sprites, portraits and elaborate effects are optional, not baseline.

## Sourcing options (prior discussion; verify before purchase)

* AaronSprites generation packs: earlier research reported 242 creatures across eight packs at roughly \$65 total; generally 96×96, commercial use and modification allowed, subject to each pack's license. Strong prototype candidate.  
* Willibab Retromon Bundle: earlier research reported 200+ sprites for about \$19.99, with attribution requirements. Free sample pack reported 53 creatures plus variants.  
* DadddyDarko Creature Collector 500: earlier discussion reported 500 static sprites, metadata and a \$5.99 listing; animated collection reported 350 creatures at \$9.99 and disclosed AI assistance. Review provenance, consistency and rights before any commercial use.  
* RetroPokeSprites commission reference: earlier advertised rates \$20 for a single sprite, \$75 for six and \$10 additional; supplied references and small retro sprites. Scarloxy, Pixmarin and other pixel artists were also identified as possible candidates. Actual original design and exclusive commercial rights may cost more.

Commission estimate for Emerald-era static originals: \$15–35 per monster in a bulk order; \$25–50 for stronger consistency and design work; \$50+ for more elaborate concept work, revisions or rights. At \$20–40 each, 30 designs cost \$600–1,200; 50 cost \$1,000–2,000; 100 cost \$2,000–4,000; 150 cost \$3,000–6,000. These are planning assumptions, not quotes. Earlier broad target: \$2,000–4,000 for a strong early roster; a more polished 100-design release can exceed \$5,000.

After the release gate only: trial five original sprites, choose a consistent artist/style guide, then commission in batches of 20–25 by evolutionary family. Negotiate commercial use, exclusivity/copyright assignment, derivative and modification rights, editable source files, transparent PNGs, revision terms and artist credit. Ensure no infringing lookalikes or undisclosed asset reuse.

## Content multiplier

Use one base design per species/evolution and programmatically produce clearly differentiated palette variants (e.g., shiny, shadow, radiant) when art direction permits. 30 designs × 5 total visual forms \= 150 collectible appearances; 100 × 8 \= 800\. These are mathematical examples, not launch requirements. Genetics, abilities and traits create meaningful mechanical variety without new illustrations. Do not confuse color variants with unique species.

Use structured design entries for monsters, items, abilities, recipes, encounters and zones. Reuse mechanics rather than custom scripting each species. AI may assist with draft descriptions and naming, with human review. Purchased placeholder art is nonexclusive; original commissions are preferable for eventual IP identity. Verify storefront AI-content disclosures and asset licensing at the appropriate time.

# 7\. Monetization direction

Owner endorsed the revised IdleOn-inspired approach. The intended experience is free-to-play, ad-free, cross-platform and free of mandatory subscriptions, with permanent account value as the initial commercial model. Protect monster trading and ranked PvP. Functional purchases should have meaningful gameplay-equivalent unlock paths; a theoretically obtainable but impractically slow path is still a competitive advantage.

## Lessons from IdleOn

Earlier research reported \$4.99 Auto Loot and Starter packs, gem packages around \$1.99–19.99, and recurring special bundles around \$12.99–34.99. IdleOn sells permanent account upgrades, convenience and compounding bonuses, while also distributing some gems through play. Its premium companion systems and strong paid bonuses are controversial; sustained popularity is not evidence that all players approve, and actual revenue/profit was not verified.

Adopt its low-friction first purchase, permanent value, earned premium currency and periodic supporter bundles. Do not copy its most aggressive paid power, paid rare creatures or randomized premium companions. Idle Buds has a more sensitive direct creature economy and ranked competition.

## Candidate shop (prices are proposals)

* \$4.99 starter supporter pack; \$9.99 advanced trainer pack; optional \$14.99 lifetime-style supporter upgrade if the benefit is compelling.  
* \$2.99–4.99 storage or organization upgrades, with a substantial free allowance and attainable in-game alternatives; extra team presets, UI themes and cosmetic customizations.  
* \$14.99–24.99 major update supporter bundles; approximately \$29.99 anniversary pack. Premium currency may be sold at several direct price points and earned through normal play.  
* Account-bound cosmetics: creature costumes, battle auras, capture effects, trainer avatars, profile banners, interface themes, egg shells and arena backgrounds. Do not sell naturally occurring rare variants as cosmetics.  
* Avoid: direct gold sales, tradable premium currency, paid exclusive monsters, stronger genetics, faster breeding, extra productive/breeding slots, paid shiny odds, paid XP/production multipliers, premium randomized eggs and mobile rewarded ads.

No mandatory subscription initially. An optional cosmetic membership was discussed (\$4.99/month) but is deferred until there is enough recurring cosmetic value. Cosmetics and functional upgrades should be reviewed for economic effects; market analytics and breeding optimization tools can themselves confer advantage. Keep essential information accessible to free players.

## Cross-platform and business constraints

Desktop-first, responsive web UI and mobile support. Proposed initial delivery is one responsive browser application/PWA, not separate native implementations. Later Steam and iOS/Android distribution should share accounts, progression, economy and entitlements. Plan payments in line with current platform requirements; browser payment provider, Steam Wallet, Apple IAP and Google Play Billing were candidate channels. Verify rules, fees, regional exceptions and account linking before launch.

Illustrative model only: 10,000 monthly active players × 5% paying × \$10 monthly average payer spend \= \$5,000 gross per month. This is not a forecast, and excludes platform/payment fees, hosting, support, taxes, art, marketing and refunds. Actual conversion and retention require evidence. Free access and fair competitive economics matter more than squeezing initial purchases.

# 8\. Scope discipline and future sequencing

This project is better aligned with the owner's data systems and engineering skills than a deep autonomous simulation such as Harem King or asset-heavy BEAT BAES. An interface-first game eliminates movement, world rendering, rigging and most custom animation. The complete live-service MMO is still ambitious: persistent transactional integrity, economy balancing, anti-cheat, support, community moderation and ongoing content are significant costs. Easier to start does not mean easier to finish.

## Proposed bounded release scope (not yet approved for implementation)

* 30 original monster designs including evolutions; five interconnected skills; catching and collection; simple genetics and breeding; automated PvE; offline progression.  
* Persistent accounts; individual-monster and resource marketplaces; asynchronous PvP; responsive desktop/mobile web UI.  
* No synchronous PvP, roguelite, complex guild wars, custom engine, native mobile clients, extensive housing, open world, long story campaign or complex genetics before the first validated release.

## Post-gate validation milestones

* First: prove the core is fun with roughly 15–20 placeholder monsters, limited skills and automated battles. Proposed 30-day / \$150 / 5–10 hours weekly experiment, only after BEAT BAES demo release. Pass if the owner returns voluntarily for seven days and multiple independent testers want to return.  
* Second: prove the economy works with persistent accounts, simple breeding and markets. Pass if players voluntarily trade and specialize, even at low player counts.  
* Third: prove competitive retention with asynchronous PvP. Pass if breeding, trading and team experimentation generate meaningful repeat play without paid power.  
* Fourth: expand only when evidence justifies it. Synchronous battles, clans, group bosses, tournaments, native clients and expeditions remain later options.

Technical concept only: simple modular application, relational database, server-authoritative state and shared auto-battle rules. Avoid microservices, elaborate infrastructure or custom engines until real needs exist. No architecture is committed yet. Earlier budget comparison suggested Idle Buds may have lower upfront cash costs than BEAT BAES but higher ongoing obligations; comparative budget ranges were theoretical and excluded owner labor.

## 12.1 Governance, business intent and product identity

The owner intends Idle Buds as a lasting creative and commercial outlet rather than a succession of games. The idle-first constraint wins when competing systems conflict. Monster collection is a central source of utility, emotion and strategy; the persistent multiplayer economy and social competition give individual achievement lasting meaning. Current working title Idle Buds supersedes Fofofadi for planning, without final commercial naming clearance. The owner wants to avoid starting and overengineering another game before shipping BEAT BAES. Adopted the universal Game Development Constitution v1.0: finish one game first, fun before engineering, ideas are not commitments, fixed release scope, timeboxed experiments, build for today, generalize only from evidence, reuse before creating, depth before breadth, play before expanding, done means playable, ship before perfection. All ideas go first to the Idea Incubator; qualify fit, smallest version/cost, priority; only explicit owner promotion enters current design. Promotion does not approve a feature for release or implementation. BEAT BAES is the sole active implementation project until its demo is publicly playable. Idle Buds research, interviews, static mockups and design documents are permitted; game code, working sandbox/prototypes, repositories, production artwork, asset/model training, infrastructure, and implementation agents are not. Do not use planning to circumvent the gate. One main design question at a time, at most two ordinary follow-ups; defer exact recipes, numbers and implementation until appropriate. The owner prefers a horizontally complete but vertically restrained MVP over an isolated prototype; release scope is not yet fixed.

## 12.2 Reference hierarchy and foundations

The original Koltera \+ Idle Clans \+ Melvor concept evolved into a specific reference hierarchy. Palworld is primary for the overall creature system: species identity, individual variation, aptitude, capture, training, breeding and productive utility. Melvor Idle/RuneScape inspire player skill progression and efficient interface-first gameplay. Idle Clans informs economy, community and social systems. Super Auto Pets informs the actual readable automatic battle rules, positioning, triggers and synergy rather than just a visual theme. Koltera remains a secondary collecting/breeding reference. IdleOn informs longevity and low-friction permanent-value monetization. Later MTG color identity and commander fantasy became conceptual references for typing and trainer participation; do not import MTG mana, deck construction or Commander rules. References are inspirations, not copied systems or assets.

Three pillars: progress at your own pace, meaningful multiplayer, and the accepted concept of monsters as partners in progress (wording provisional). The owner did not want earning a first monster itself to be the third pillar; it is an onboarding principle. Idle gameplay must feel satisfying even before acquiring a Bud. Players may choose collector, producer, merchant, breeder, battler or mixed play, without classes or professions locked by selection. Specialization comes from time, skill and decisions. Broad self-sufficiency is possible; cooperation and trade are generally more efficient. The owner's 80/20 framing is a heuristic for casual accessibility and solo access, not a measured market split or fixed quota. Dedicated optimization can be deep, but spreadsheets are not required for the ordinary player.

Daily loop: assign → progress while away → inspect rewarding outcomes → choose goals → reassign. Target 5–10 minutes for normal daily check-ins; players who want to stay longer should have meaningful activities. Intrinsic satisfaction (numbers improve), anticipation (catches, eggs, sales, battles) and ambition (personal and social goals) all matter. Long-term direction roughly 60–70% self-renewing emergent systems and 20–30% authored content, illustrative rather than exhaustive. Veteran effort, wealth, collections and prestige should matter without a permanently dominant head start; new players need plausible routes to meaningful competition. Endless opportunities need not imply endless vertical power. Exact catch-up and active-play balance remain open.

## 12.3 Initial six-question player-journey interview

1\. Onboarding: teach through real gameplay and goal-driven discovery, transitioning gradually to contextual/open sandbox play. No mandatory lengthy tutorial path. Within roughly 10–15 minutes, understand the loop, earn the first Bud and arrange meaningful offline progress. This is a satisfying logout opportunity, not a session limit. Fast initial progression should expand capabilities and opportunities rather than merely increasing timer durations.

2\. Acquisition: earn the first Bud through trainer-capable combat. Choose a hunting location, then encounter a species from a small eligible pool with randomized individual qualities; each possible first Bud must be useful. Wild catches require combat; a solo player-character can fight before owning a Bud. Breeding and trade are alternate acquisition paths. Deliberate species hunting and surprising specimens must coexist; exact capture timing, odds and mechanics remain open.

3\. Production: personal node-based activities, one entity per node (Tender or Bud), no shared-node worker stacking or global node competition. Deploy Buds simultaneously in the same or different skills subject to progression-limited capacity. Tender skill unlocks resource tier; species aptitude determines work eligibility and effectiveness. No duplicate per-Bud skilling grind. Later Q1–Q6 develop this.

4\. Combat: one automatic battle system, inspired by SAP, plus meaningful Palworld-style personal participation. Trainer/Tender-focused, Bud-focused and hybrid builds must all be plausible. Clever ordinary teams should be able to beat costly exceptional specimens. No locked combat classes. Later combat interview develops specific foundations.

5\. Breeding: accessible relatively early but deliberate slow-burn mastery for professional breeders. Breeding offers more consistent outcomes, not an automatically higher ceiling than wild catching. Hunters supply desirable fresh specimens; breeders refine/combinate; collectors, producers and battlers create demand. Keep inheritance approachable and preserve ongoing wild value; avoid infinite perfectly reproducible supply. No exact egg timer, parent rules, breeding costs, or separate breeding skill approved.

6\. Multiplayer: immediately make community and markets visible, then unlock participation naturally and early while preserving earned first Bud. Optional prestigious PvP, asynchronous first, synchronous later; mainly cosmetic/recognition rewards (titles and variants as examples). PvP must be easy to try but never compulsory to normal advancement. Preserve shared rules and a fair route for newer players. Exact ranks, matchmaking, rewards, cooperation and guild mechanisms unresolved.

## 12.4 Marketplace and monetization

Player-to-player trading is the primary economy; open trading by default, with carefully justified account-bound exceptions. Purchasing a powerful Bud should not bypass Tender progression or resource access. Basic progression remains feasible without market liquidity; cooperation and commerce improve efficiency. Initially player-only marketplace, then owner raised a hybrid NPC general store. Pinned candidate: NPC buys eligible ordinary goods at simple baseline values and sells only stock, combining player-supplied inventory and bounded essential restock. NPC sell price higher than buy price (illustration: copper bought at 2 gold, sold at 10, player exchange at 5). Item removal at high stock is a potential sink. The NPC should provide liquidity and essentials without supplanting player trade. Assistant recommended excluding Buds, rare collectibles and premium items, not owner-approved. Unlimited buyback can create currency inflation even when stock is capped; qualify buyback controls, item/currency sinks, eligibility and restocking later. This entire candidate remains HOLD, not silently promoted. Unified marketplace and player-funded buy orders remain candidates.

Monetization changed from earlier restrictive/cosmetic-oriented proposals toward owner-approved IdleOn-like low-friction \~\$5 first-purchase hypothesis, affordable one-time permanent upgrades, earnable premium currency and supporter/update bundles, without mandatory subscription. Paid acceleration is expressly acceptable if all features and completion remain genuinely reachable through free play. Exact shop, caps, rates and allowable acceleration effects on market supply/PvP need design and evidence. Earned prestige is not a product to sell. Historical log's no-paid-acceleration and narrow five-skill proposals are superseded where they conflict; historical prices and conversion examples are not forecasts or commitments.

No purchases or licenses verified as part of this checkpoint. Earlier 30–50 sprite dataset and costs were exploratory.
