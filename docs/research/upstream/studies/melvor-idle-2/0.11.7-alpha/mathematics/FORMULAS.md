## Model results (`simulations/m2_model.py`)
- **Woodcutting is health-based**: a Normal Tree (3 HP, 1 s hits) gives a log every 3 s; Redwood (26 HP) every 26 s. Base XP/h rises from 3 600 to ~23 000–31 000 at the top trees; bushes (2 HP) give quick, low-XP logs.
- **Mining has built-in bad-luck protection**: a Stone rock (p = 0.40) takes 1.96 hits on average (a pure geometric roll would average 2.5) and never more than 3; Jadestone (p = 0.02) averages 31.8 hits with a cap of 50 instead of 50 on average with a long tail. The mechanic lowers the mean as well as cutting the tail; break-chance bonuses shorten both the mean and the cap.
- **Thieving** perceptions are lower than in Melvor 1 at the start (Citizen 32 → 76 % at level 1, 100 % from level ~32) and climb to 3 580 (Palace Vault, 25 stages, 5.4 % at level 99), so stealth modifiers again carry the late game. Because XP arrives once per completed heist, base XP/h at 99 stays in a narrow 8 000–22 000 band across targets (Lower Floors ≈ 20 900, Palace Vault ≈ 10 000, before stuns).
- **Combat**: hit chance unchanged from Melvor 1; resistance now comes from a protection-vs-power ratio (equal values → 50 %) with a 70 % cap; area darkness cuts the player's own accuracy and evasion (to the minimum of 1 at 20 points), not the enemy's.
- **Pets** are tied to XP, not actions: with ScaleFactor 13 034 431 a pet is expected once per 13.03 M XP, so P(pet by 99) = 63.2 %, P(by 104.3 M XP) ≈ 100 %; the 104.3 M-scale master pets are not wired in this build.
- **Offline** at 60 Hz is 5 184 000 ticks for 24 h (Melvor 1: 1 728 000 at 20 Hz), executed off the main thread.
